# Product and Business-Model Playbooks

Use after selecting the primary workbench. These are proof and asset overlays, not complete strategies.

## Beauty and personal care

Prioritize exact pack/shade, texture/application, routine fit, skin/material behavior, ingredient/mechanism evidence, creator demonstration, and compliant claims. Useful assets: packshot set, swatch/texture, application macro, routine, shade/variant guide, ingredient/process proof, UGC, campaign beauty image. Reject plastic skin, fake clinical diagrams, unsupported before/after, or invented ingredients.

## Fashion, footwear, and accessories

Prioritize fit, silhouette, construction, fabric movement, size, detail, styling range, care, and provenance when confirmed. Useful assets: clean front/back, on-body fit, movement, detail macro, scale, styling combinations, size guide, collection lineup, editorial campaign. Reject lens-made proportions, cloned models, or hidden fit limitations.

## Jewelry and luxury goods

Prioritize material, finish, scale on body, closure, craft, provenance, packaging, service, and authenticity evidence. Use controlled reflection, macro, on-body scale, packaging/unboxing, craft/process, editorial still life, and press-ready imagery. Never invent gemstone, metal, origin, rarity, or certification.

## Food, beverage, and CPG

Prioritize pack recognition, appetite, texture, preparation, serving, occasion, ingredients, shelf behavior, trial, and repeat purchase. Use hero pack, real serving, process, ingredient/material, scale, multipack, retail/POS, delivery-app thumbnail, and seasonal assets. Reject fake steam, impossible splashes, or misleading serving size.

## Electronics and appliances

Prioritize exact device geometry, ports, interface, dimensions, setup, compatibility, use case, performance evidence, included items, and support/warranty when confirmed. Use clean multi-angle set, interface capture, scale, installation, feature demo, comparison, accessories, environment, and explainer. Do not generate fake UI or specifications.

## Home, furniture, and decor

Prioritize dimensions, room scale, materials, assembly, care, storage/capacity, color, and delivery. Use cutout, room context, scale, material macro, alternate configuration, dimensions, assembly, collection lineup, and seasonal room stories. Prevent wide-angle distortion and misleading room size.

## SaaS, apps, and digital products

Prioritize real workflow, interface artifacts, time/risk change, collaboration, integration, onboarding, security/compliance evidence, and customer outcomes. Use actual screenshots, annotated flows, demo video, before/after process, case study, comparison, webinar, founder/expert POV, sales deck, and lifecycle onboarding. Never generate a fake product UI as proof.

## Services and local businesses

Prioritize service packaging, process, people, tools, location, qualification, response expectations, case evidence, reviews with permission, booking/quote path, and local trust. Use real staff/site/tools, process sequence, completed work, FAQ, service comparison, local landing pages, Google Business Profile-compatible media, lead form, and follow-up. Reject generic stock teams and fake badges.

## Hospitality, travel, and experiences

Prioritize place, room/spatial truth, amenities, service, arrival, food, local context, packages, seasonality, availability, and booking terms. Use room/venue sequence, map/context, service moment, itinerary, event, UGC, OTA/direct-booking derivatives, and press gallery. Avoid generic destination stereotypes and misleading room perspectives.

## Education, knowledge products, and personal brands

Prioritize curriculum/artifact, teaching method, expertise evidence, sample lesson, learner work, community behavior, fit, time commitment, and verified outcomes. Use annotated material, lesson clips, founder/expert content, syllabus, case study, webinar, lead magnet, launch, sales page, and lifecycle nurture. Reject fake income, enrollment, or transformation claims.

## Art, handmade, collectibles, and cultural products

Prioritize authorship, material, scale, process, edition/provenance, installation/use context, packaging, and story. Use clean documentation, macro, process, studio portrait, installation, editorial interpretation, collection/edition guide, press kit, and collector-facing sales material. Distinguish documentary images from conceptual campaign art.

## Wellness and fitness

Prioritize routine adherence, coaching method, equipment reality, recovery, body diversity, and outcomes only where they were actually measured. Use real effort and real rest, the room the work happens in, coach interaction, progression across weeks rather than a single after, and equipment being used rather than posed with. Reject beige serenity, the unsupported before/after pair, impossible bodies, and pseudo-medical phrasing. This category and personal care are where an unsupported transformation claim stops being a marketing decision and becomes a claims problem, so anything implying a health outcome goes through `claims-proof-ledger.md` before it ships, not after.

## Where the work happens

Every section above answers what to shoot and none of them answers where, which is the gap that sends a brief back with a correct asset list and a studio nobody believes. Location is not decoration. It is most of what makes an asset read as evidence rather than as advertising.

Shoot where the product is actually used, at the hour it is actually used. For personal care that is a bathroom with a window and a backstage touch-up, not a pastel pedestal. For food it is hands mid-process and a table that has been eaten off. For SaaS it is the actual interface in sequence, and for local services it is the site, in whatever weather the crew is working in. Wellness wants the gym when it is busy.

Two categories break the rule for a reason worth knowing. Jewelry and luxury goods are bought partly on presentation, so a controlled still life is truthful there in a way it is not elsewhere. Art documentation has to be neutral by function: a documentary image of a work is not a campaign image of it, and conflating the two misrepresents what the buyer receives.

## Playbook selection test

Before committing to any of the above, state four things:

1. The category reflex being rejected.
2. The product-specific mechanism replacing it.
3. The proof of that mechanism visible in the asset.
4. The channel behaviour the idea supports.

If the second and third cannot be written concretely, the playbook is being used as a recipe, which is the failure this file is most often responsible for. A category playbook is a list of what the category already looks like. Read as a shopping list it reproduces the saturated look by construction. Read as a rejection list it earns its place.

## What this file is not

The category is not the audience. Two serum brands selling to different buyers need different work from the same paragraph above, and nothing here settles that — `customer-evidence.md` does.

Retail and marketplace mechanics are also not a product category, however often they are written as one. Listing quality, variant range, thumbnail behaviour and channel crops belong to the `Commerce and merchandising` section below and apply across every category here. A beauty brand selling on TikTok Shop has a commerce problem wearing a beauty problem's clothes, and reaching for the beauty paragraph first is how that gets missed.

## Commerce and merchandising

### Commerce decision sequence

Build PDP, marketplace, catalog, and retail assets to answer:

1. What exactly is it?
2. Is it relevant to my situation?
3. Why is this version worth choosing?
4. What evidence reduces risk?
5. Which variant, size, bundle, or plan is right?
6. What is included, delivered, or required?
7. What happens after purchase?

Do not treat ecommerce as a campaign poster plus a buy button.

### Minimum media sequence

Adapt order to category and platform:

1. Main product recognition frame.
2. Alternate angles or interface states.
3. Scale, fit, capacity, or room context.
4. Material, texture, construction, or detail macro.
5. Product in use with a believable hand/body/environment.
6. Mechanism or feature proof.
7. Variant, collection, or selection guide.
8. Bundle and what's included.
9. Instructions, care, compatibility, or dimensions.
10. Trust/proof asset when substantiated.

Use `assets/registries/asset-formats.json` instead of inventing a new shot list each time.

### Listing copy contract

Create only from confirmed truth:

- Search-aware title without keyword stuffing.
- One-line value proposition.
- Benefit bullets tied to mechanism or specification.
- Product description and use context.
- Variant, size, material, compatibility, care, and included-items data.
- FAQ and objection handling.
- Shipping, returns, warranty, price, availability, and promotion only when confirmed.
- Alt text and captions that describe the actual image.

### Marketplace and retailer adaptation

- Read current marketplace image, text, prohibited-claim, and category requirements before export.
- Keep a clean source master and placement-specific derivatives.
- Separate platform-required main image from editorial/lifestyle secondary images.
- Keep SKU names, color, scale, labels, and included accessories consistent across catalog, ads, and PDP.
- Treat Amazon/DSP/Stores, Google Merchant, retailer media, and local marketplaces as distinct placements.

### Merchandising system

For SKU families record:

- Parent/child relationship.
- Variant axis: color, flavor, size, finish, plan, bundle, or compatibility.
- Shared camera, background, crop, scale, and shadow rules.
- Which differences must be immediately visible at thumbnail size.
- Bundle logic and cross-sell/upsell relationship.
- Seasonal refresh rules without changing product truth.

### Return-reduction QA

Reject when assets hide or misrepresent:

- Size, fit, material, color, finish, capacity, compatibility, assembly, contents, or care.
- Product limitations or required accessories.
- Label, ingredient, safety, warranty, shipping, or return information.
- Perspective that makes a product appear larger or slimmer than it is.
- Lifestyle props that look included but are not.

Measure listing CTR, media engagement, add-to-cart, PDP conversion, AOV, variant selection, and return reasons. Do not optimize CTR by increasing ambiguity.
