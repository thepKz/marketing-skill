# Vietnam Market & Channel Context — Operating Dossier for Small F&B and Consumer Brands

## What this is for

You are about to make marketing decisions for a Vietnamese business — most likely a small food-and-beverage
operation (a bún bò shop, a coffee kiosk, a bakery, a bottled-sauce brand) or a small consumer brand — and you
are not a marketer, and possibly not Vietnamese. This dossier gives you the operating context a local marketer
would carry in their head: which channel does which job, how influencer work actually gets bought here, what
customers expect at checkout, how to write a price so it does not look foreign, how to write copy that does not
read like a translation, when the money moves during the year, and where the advertising law will bite you.
Every number carries a source URL and a retrieval date, or is explicitly marked as unverified. Read the evidence
markers: they are the most important thing in the file.

### 1.2 Channel-by-channel job assignment

This is the operational core of the section. Each channel has one primary job. Using a channel for the wrong job
is how small F&B brands waste their entire budget.

| Channel | Primary job | Secondary job | What it is NOT for | Failure mode if you get it wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Facebook Page + Groups** | Local discovery and social proof at neighbourhood scale; the default "does this business exist and is it real" check | Paid reach to a tight radius; comment-based ordering | Brand-building through polished campaign assets | You post studio-grade content nobody engages with; the algorithm buries the Page; your actual customers never find you because you were not in the local food group |
| **Facebook Messenger** | Order-taking and Q&A for shops without an app | Retargeting via click-to-Messenger ads | Bulk broadcast | You treat inbox as a broadcast list, get reported, lose the ability to reply within the messaging window |
| **Zalo (personal + OA)** | Retention, order confirmation, loyalty, re-order nudges; the channel with the highest chance of actually being *read* | Local group coordination (apartment buildings, offices, schools) | Cold acquisition; discovery | You try to acquire strangers on Zalo. Zalo OA can only message users who follow or have interacted with it — see §1.4. Cold Zalo outreach is spam and now has a legal edge (§8) |
| **TikTok (organic + Shop)** | Demand *creation* — making people want a dish they had not thought about; the fastest path from zero followers to real reach | Direct commerce for packaged goods (TikTok Shop) | Reliable steady-state reach; long-lived evergreen assets | You post one video, it flops, you conclude "TikTok doesn't work". TikTok is a volume game: many attempts, short cycles |
| **Instagram** | Aesthetic positioning for premium urban concepts; a portfolio you point press and partners at | Reaching expats, high-income HCMC/Hanoi 18–30 F | Volume acquisition anywhere in Vietnam | You spend your launch budget reaching 11.7m of 85.6m internet users, skewed away from your actual price point |
| **YouTube** | Long-form credibility (founder story, process, "how the broth is made"); search-durable content | Pre-roll for brand awareness at low CPM | Impulse conversion | You expect a 12-minute video to drive Tuesday-lunch orders |
| **Shopee / TikTok Shop / Lazada** | Selling *shelf-stable, shippable* product (sauces, dried noodles, coffee beans, gift boxes) | Price discovery; review accumulation | Selling prepared food | You list a perishable dish, cannot fulfil, take a rating hit that is very hard to undo |
| **GrabFood / ShopeeFood / beFood** | Incremental order volume from customers who will never walk in; discovery inside a hungry, high-intent context | Trial of new dishes at a discount | Building a margin-healthy core business | You let apps become >50% of revenue at 20–30% commission and price-war pressure, and your unit economics collapse (§4.3) |

### 1.3 Facebook: groups are the real channel

`[structural]` The high-leverage Facebook surface for a small Vietnamese F&B business is **not** the Page — it
is the community group. Vietnamese Facebook is organised into a dense mesh of hyperlocal and interest groups:
residential-compound groups, district food groups ("review đồ ăn <district>"), office-building lunch groups,
university groups, hobby and parenting groups. These are where discovery and social proof happen and where
"seeding" (§3.5) operates.

Supporting evidence for the community mechanism, `[verified]` (source:
https://vietnamnet.vn/en/vietnamese-consumers-rely-on-online-reviews-before-spending-2474022.html, retrieved
2026-07-29, figures attributed to DoubleVerify *Global Insights 2025*):

- 59% follow online communities (gaming groups, parenting forums, lifestyle collectives)
- 48% buy products based on positive community feedback
- 39% have purchased products promoted within online communities
- **37% trust community recommendations more than celebrity endorsements**
- 48% read online reviews before committing to a purchase
- 42% use social media to seek out and verify product information
- 39% frequently watch product review / tutorial videos on YouTube, TikTok, Facebook
- Macro-influencer (100K–1M followers) engagement rate reported at 39%; 32% say mega-influencers (1M+) influence
  purchase decisions

Decision rule: **if your budget is under the equivalent of a few million đồng per month, spend it on being
genuinely present and well-reviewed in 5–15 relevant local groups before you spend a single đồng on a boosted
post.** What breaks if you ignore this: you buy reach to people with no reason to trust you, in a market where
37% explicitly trust their community over celebrities.

`[UNVERIFIED - would need a platform-published or reputable-research figure on Vietnamese Facebook Group
membership/engagement volume]` I have no citable number for how many Vietnamese Facebook users are active in
local commerce/food groups. Treat the group mechanism as a well-attested qualitative pattern plus the
community-trust survey numbers above, not as a sized channel.

### 1.4 Zalo: the retention utility, and its hard rules

`[structural]` Zalo is a VNG-owned Vietnamese messenger that functions as the country's default
person-to-person and business-to-person channel. For a marketer it splits into three things:

1. **Personal Zalo** — how a shop owner actually talks to regulars. Unscalable, extremely high trust.
2. **Zalo Official Account (OA)** — the business identity. Followers ("người quan tâm"), broadcast, chat.
3. **ZNS (Zalo Notification Service)** — templated transactional/notification messages sent to a *phone number*
   via API, i.e. it can reach people who do not follow your OA, but only for permitted notification purposes.

Message types and their conditions, `[verified]` (source:
https://oa.zalo.me/home/documents/guides/tong-quan-cac-loai-tin-nhan-tren-zalo-official-account-_3651713298729094511,
retrieved 2026-07-29):

| Zalo OA message type | Who you may send to | Time window | Cost / quota |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tin Tư vấn** (advisory / support reply) | Users who have interacted with the OA and have not blocked it | OA Manager: within 365 days of last interaction; OpenAPI: within 7 days | Free within 48 hours of the user's interaction; charged beyond 48 hours |
| **Tin Broadcast** (tin Truyền thông) | Users who *follow* the OA | **06:00–19:59 only** | Quota limited by the OA's service package tier |
| **GMF group messages** | Members of the OA's GMF chat groups | 24/7 | Free |
| **ZBS Template messages** — split into *tin Giao dịch* (transaction) and *tin Hậu mãi* (retention/after-sales) | Addressable by User ID or phone number, via OpenAPI or OA Manager | per template category | paid |

Corroborating detail on broadcast targeting and the 06:00–19:59 send window (by age, gender, geography, OS and
assigned tags, within quota), and the rule that an OA can only message a user who has proactively interacted
with or permitted the OA: `[search-level]` from Zalo's own policy page
(https://oa.zalo.me/home/resources/news/thong-bao-chinh-sach-gui-tin-va-quy-dinh-phi-gui-tin_1433049880779375099)
and the Zalo OA support article
(https://zalooa.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/64000261136-) — both seen in search summaries only;
the pages are JS-rendered and I could not read them directly. Re-check before designing a messaging cadence.

**ZNS pricing:** `[UNVERIFIED - needs the current official ZNS price table from zalo.cloud or Zalo's OA
business portal, or a quote from a ZNS reseller]` The structure is documented — price varies by template
category, number and type of CTA buttons, inclusion of images, and send time, with the first CTA free and
pay-per-successfully-delivered-message — but I found no official price figures I could read. Do not put a
per-message cost in a budget until you have the table. Anyone quoting you a flat "X đồng per ZNS" is quoting one
cell of a matrix.

Decision rules for Zalo:

- **Collect the phone number at the first transaction.** In Vietnam the phone number *is* the Zalo identity.
  Without it you have no retention channel. What breaks if you skip it: you are permanently dependent on paid
  acquisition and on delivery-app-owned customers whose contact details you never see.
- **Never broadcast after 19:59.** It is not permitted for broadcast and, culturally, evening messaging from a
  business reads as intrusive. What breaks: send failure, or a wave of unfollows.
- **Use *tin Giao dịch* for order/delivery/booking facts and *tin Hậu mãi* for win-back — do not smuggle
  promotions into transactional templates.** What breaks: template rejection, OA quality downgrade, and under
  the 2026 data-protection law (§8.4) a consent problem.

### 1.5 TikTok: the demand-creation engine, and its two distinct surfaces

`[structural]` Treat TikTok-as-media and TikTok Shop as two different businesses that happen to share an app.

- **TikTok-as-media** is where a Vietnamese F&B brand can go from zero to real reach fastest, because
  distribution is content-led rather than follower-led. It is the right channel for: a dish nobody knows,
  a visual process (pulling noodles, ladling broth, cracking a bánh flan), a location reveal, a price reveal.
- **TikTok Shop** is a marketplace with commission and fulfilment obligations (§4.2). It is right for
  shelf-stable product and wrong for prepared food.

TikTok's Vietnam reach is 76.1 million adults 18+, and note TikTok reports only 18+ `[verified]`
(datareportal, above). Weekly-usage cross-check: 67% `[verified]` (DoubleVerify via VietnamNet, above).

`[UNVERIFIED - needs a TikTok-published or reputable-research figure]` I have no citable figure for the share of
Vietnamese TikTok viewing that is food/F&B content, nor for the typical view-to-footfall conversion for a single
outlet. Do not let anyone sell you a "TikTok drives X% of restaurant discovery in Vietnam" number without a
source.

1. **Lazada is no longer a channel decision for a small brand.** At ~3% combined with Tiki, listing there is

   execute all four badly.
2. **The number of selling shops fell 7.43% while GMV grew 34.75%** `[verified]`. The market is consolidating

   32.6% of GMV). Plan for that: differentiate on product, not on being present.
3. **~8% of all retail is online on two platforms** `[verified]`. For prepared food and beverage, the
   overwhelming majority of money is still offline or in the delivery apps. Do not let e-commerce enthusiasm

Five things to do with those numbers:

1. **Scale calibration.** ~329,500 outlets against a population of 102 million is roughly one F&B outlet per
   ~310 people. `[verified]` inputs, arithmetic mine. This is an extraordinarily dense, low-switching-cost
   market. Your differentiation cannot be "we exist and we are nearby" — a competitor is also nearby.
2. **Growth is single-digit while e-commerce is +34.75%.** `[verified]` F&B revenue grew 5.5%; outlet count grew
   2.0%. Anyone promising you category-level tailwinds is wrong. Growth has to come from share.
3. **The 35.000đ+ beverage bracket holding 57.58% share** `[verified]` is your anchor for drinks pricing. A
   35.000–45.000đ drink is mainstream, not premium. Pricing a signature drink at 25.000đ signals "cheap", not
   "value", in urban markets.
4. **69.38% of operators are squeezed by input costs** `[verified]`. This means discount-led competition is
   being fought by businesses that cannot afford it. Do not enter a price war you did not start; compete on
   speed (Rakuten's 47%/26% finding, §1.7) and on trust.
5. **Food-safety incidents move over half the market** `[verified]` (54.45% for Canfoco). Food-safety
   communication is not a compliance chore in Vietnam; it is a live marketing asset. Photograph your
   provenance, show your certificates, name your suppliers. What breaks if you ignore this: when the next
   category-wide scare happens, you have nothing to say and you lose trade you never lose back.

## 3. KOL and KOC practice, and how it differs from Western influencer marketing

### 3.1 The vocabulary, precisely

`[structural]` Vietnamese practice uses a taxonomy imported from Chinese-market usage, not from Western
"influencer / nano / micro / macro" framing. Getting the words right matters because it is how briefs, quotes
and invoices are written.

| Term | Vietnamese usage | Rough Western analogue | What they are actually bought for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **KOL** — Key Opinion Leader | Anyone with meaningful public standing: celebrity, singer, actor, athlete, large-audience creator, credentialed expert | Celebrity / macro influencer | Awareness, legitimacy, PR value, campaign centrepiece |
| **KOC** — Key Opinion Consumer | An ordinary consumer with a small-to-mid audience who reviews things they have bought/used | Micro/nano influencer, but with a *reviewer* identity rather than a *creator* identity | Conversion, review volume, "real person" proof |
| **KOS** — Key Opinion Sales | A seller-creator whose content is explicitly a sales channel (heavy on livestream) | Live-commerce host / affiliate | Direct GMV |
| **Người chuyển tải sản phẩm quảng cáo** | The *legal* term introduced by the amended Advertising Law for anyone who directly advertises, recommends or endorses a product online or by wearing/displaying it for profit | "Endorser" in FTC terms | This is the term that creates legal duties — see §8.2 |
| **Seeding** | Placing seemingly-organic posts/comments in groups and forums, usually paid or coordinated | Astroturfing / community seeding | Volume of apparent word-of-mouth |
| **Booking** | The verb for hiring a KOL/KOC. "Booking KOC" is standard business Vietnamese. | "Talent booking" | — |
| **Review** | Used as a Vietnamese noun/verb. "Video review", "đi review quán" | Review | — |

The `[verified]` legal definition of *người chuyển tải sản phẩm quảng cáo* — "người trực tiếp quảng cáo,
khuyến nghị, xác nhận sản phẩm, hàng hóa, dịch vụ trên mạng hoặc trực tiếp quảng cáo bằng hình thức mặc, treo,
gắn, dán, vẽ, sử dụng có mục đích sinh lợi hoặc hình thức khác theo quy định của Chính phủ" — is
`[search-level]` as an exact quote (I saw it in a search summary of plo.vn and brandsvietnam commentary, not in

### 3.2 Six ways Vietnamese practice genuinely differs from Western influencer marketing

`[structural]`, drawn from how the market is described across the Vietnamese trade press and agency material I
read at search level; each is a pattern claim, not a statistic.

1. **KOC is a first-class, separately-budgeted line item, not "micro-influencer overflow".** In Western
   practice, micro-influencers are usually a cheaper tier of the same buy. In Vietnam, KOC is a distinct
   *mechanism* — volume of authentic-looking reviews from ordinary consumers — often bought through agencies or
   platforms that manage hundreds of small accounts at once, with product-plus-small-fee or
   product-plus-commission compensation rather than flat fees. `[search-level]` (multiple agency sources,
   e.g. https://www.dym-vietnam.com/en/columns/influencer/what-is-a-koc-criteria-for-selecting-and-booking-kocs-effectively-for-brands/,
   https://dps.media/en/top-6-kol-koc-booking-services-suitable-for-brands/, retrieved 2026-07-29). Flag for
   re-check: these are agency marketing pages and have an interest in the framing.

2. **The stated split of purpose is explicit and widely repeated: KOL for awareness, KOC/micro for
   conversion.** `[search-level]` (same agency sources). Use it as a planning heuristic, not as evidence.

3. **Commission/affiliate compensation is normal, not exceptional.** TikTok Shop's affiliate mechanism means a
   creator can be paid purely on delivered orders. Affiliate/KOC commission is commonly described at **10–20%
   of product price, charged only when the order comes from that creator and is successfully delivered**
   `[search-level]` (source: https://www.misaeshop.vn/13131/phi-san-tiktok-shop/ and
   https://kinhdoanh.ahamove.com/cac-loai-phi-ban-hang-tren-tiktok-shop, retrieved 2026-07-29 — I fetched the
   misaeshop page and it confirmed a large fee taxonomy including affiliate/SFP items but the specific 10–20%
   band came from the search summary; re-check in TikTok Shop Seller Center). This changes negotiation: you can
   often start a KOC relationship at zero cash risk, which is almost never true of a Western macro deal.

4. **Livestream is a primary commerce format, not a novelty.** Metric attributes 2025's growth partly to a
   year-long chain of shopping events "bao gồm Tết Nguyên Đán, Sale Hè 6.6, Đại Lễ Hội 8.8, Siêu Sale 9.9,
   Ngày Độc Thân 11.11, và đặc biệt là chuỗi Sale cuối năm 12.12 kéo dài đến Black Friday" and to
   livestream-driven selling `[verified]`/`[search-level]` (source:
   https://metric.vn/insights/metric-chinh-thuc-cong-bo-bao-cao-thi-truong-thuong-mai-dien-tu-viet-nam-2025/ —
   I fetched this page and the figures in §1.6 are verified; the specific event-list sentence I have at
   search-level from the Brands Vietnam mirror of the same release).

5. **Trust sits with the community, not the celebrity.** 37% say they trust community recommendations more than
   celebrity endorsements `[verified]` (DoubleVerify via VietnamNet, cited above). Combined with 39% having
   bought something promoted inside a community, this is the empirical case for KOC-and-groups over
   celebrity-and-billboard for a small brand.

6. **From 1 January 2026 the endorser carries personal legal duties, including a tax duty.** `[verified]` — see
   §8.2. This is a genuine divergence from the FTC-disclosure-only Western norm: Vietnamese law now requires the
   endorser to have verified the advertiser, checked product documentation, and only advertise products they
   have used or genuinely understand, and to produce contracts and materials to inspectors on request.

### 3.3 What a KOL/KOC costs

`[UNVERIFIED - needs live quotes from 2-3 Vietnamese influencer platforms/agencies (e.g. Hiip, Revu Vietnam,
7SAT, KOLid) or a screenshot of a current rate card]` **I will not give you a rate card.** Published Vietnamese
influencer pricing is inconsistent, agency-sourced, and stale within a quarter. Any number I produced here would
be a fabrication risk with a direct budget consequence.

What I can give you is the *structure* of a quote `[structural]`, so you can read one:

- **Base fee** ("phí booking" / "phí KOL") — flat, per deliverable.
- **Deliverable spec** — e.g. 1 TikTok video ≥30s, 1 Facebook post, 3 Instagram Stories. Count and platform,
  not "a campaign".
- **Usage / rights window** — how long you may reuse the asset in paid media ("quyền sử dụng hình ảnh"). This is
  where Vietnamese quotes most often hide cost: a cheap base fee with 0 paid-usage rights is not cheap, because
  the asset is the thing you actually wanted.
- **Exclusivity** ("độc quyền ngành hàng") — no competing brand for N months. Priced separately.
- **Whitelisting / Spark Ads** — permission to run paid spend through their handle. Usually a separate line.
- **Commission override** — affiliate % on tracked orders.
- **Product/hosting cost** ("chi phí trải nghiệm") — meals, travel, samples. For F&B this is often the whole
  KOC deal.

**Negotiation rules that hold regardless of price level:**

- Buy **paid-usage rights on every deal**, even KOC ones. The best-performing KOC asset is worth more as an ad
  creative than as an organic post. What breaks: you find a winning video and cannot legally spend behind it.
- Insist on **screen-recorded analytics** (TikTok/Facebook creator insights), not screenshots of a number.
- Pay in **two tranches: on delivery and on 7-day performance**, where you can. This is common enough to ask
  for and filters out engagement-pod accounts.
- For F&B, require the KOC to **visit and pay-then-be-reimbursed**, or at minimum to disclose the hosting.
  Under §8.2 both of you now have exposure if the review implies an un-had experience.

### 3.4 A KOC programme you can actually run (worked example — numbers illustrative)

`[illustrative]` — **every đồng figure below is invented so the arithmetic is followable. None is a real quote.**

Scenario: a single bún bò shop in Thủ Đức, HCMC. Goal: 300 incremental first-time visits in 8 weeks.

| Line | Assumption | Value |
|---|---|---|
| KOCs booked | 20 KOCs, 5k–30k followers, TikTok-primary, HCMC food niche | 20 |
| Cost per KOC | free meal for 2 (cost to shop: 130.000đ food cost) + 300.000đ fee | 430.000đ each |
| Total programme cost | 20 × 430.000đ | **8.600.000đ** |
| Videos delivered | 1 video each, 18 delivered on time | 18 |
| Median views per video | | 4.000 |
| Two outliers | | 60.000 and 120.000 views |
| Total views | (16 × 4.000) + 180.000 | 244.000 |
| Assumed visit rate on views | 0.15% | 366 visits |
| First-visit average bill | | 85.000đ |
| Gross revenue from programme | 366 × 85.000đ | 31.110.000đ |
| Gross margin assumed | 65% | 20.221.500đ |
| Contribution after programme cost | 20.221.500đ − 8.600.000đ | **+11.621.500đ** |
| Cost per acquired first visit | 8.600.000đ ÷ 366 | **~23.500đ** |

What this worked example is really teaching, and this part is `[structural]`:

- **The outcome is decided by the two outliers, not by the median.** 180.000 of 244.000 views came from 2 of 18
  videos. A KOC programme is a portfolio bet. Booking 5 KOCs is not a smaller version of booking 20; it is a
  different, much worse bet, because you probably draw no outlier.
- **Your control variable is the number of attempts, not the quality of the brief.** Increase N before you
  increase spend per unit.
- **The single most valuable output is the right to re-run the outlier as a paid ad.** Which is why §3.3 says
  buy usage rights on every deal.
- **You must be able to measure "first visit".** Without a mechanism (a code word, a KOC-specific voucher, a
  QR landing page per creator) the whole calculation is fiction. Build the measurement before you book anyone.

### 3.5 Seeding: what it is, and the line you should not cross

`[structural]` "Seeding" in Vietnamese practice means placing posts and comments in groups and forums so that
they read as spontaneous. It is widespread and it is sold as a service by the unit (per post, per comment, per
group).

Three tiers, only the first two of which you should buy:

1. **Legitimate community participation.** You, or genuinely-paid-and-disclosed people, posting real content in
   relevant groups. Fine.
2. **Coordinated real experiences.** Real customers, incentivised and disclosed, encouraged to post. Fine if the
   incentive is disclosed.
3. **Fabricated identities and fabricated experiences.** Accounts pretending to be customers who never ate your
   food; purchased comment volume; fake reviews. **Do not.** Under the amended Advertising Law the endorser must
   ensure truthfulness and may only advertise products they have used or genuinely understand, and both
   advertiser and endorser now have documentable duties `[verified]` (§8.2). Beyond the legal exposure, in a
   market where 48% read reviews before buying and 54.45% changed behaviour after one food-safety scandal
   `[verified]`, being caught faking is a category-level credibility loss you cannot buy back.

`[UNVERIFIED - needs a citable enforcement case or a regulator statement specifically on paid fake reviews in
Vietnam]` I did not find a specific Vietnamese enforcement action against fake reviews that I could cite. The
prohibition I am relying on is the general truthfulness duty plus consumer-protection law, not a
fake-review-specific provision.

## 4. Payment, checkout and platform economics

### 4.1 What customers expect at checkout

`[structural]` Four payment behaviours coexist in Vietnam, and a small brand needs three of them.

| Method | Vietnamese name | Where it dominates | What you must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | tiền mặt | Street-level F&B, older customers, rural | Keep change. Do not go cashless as a positioning statement. |
| **VietQR bank transfer** | chuyển khoản / quét mã VietQR | In-store, takeaway, deposits, pre-orders, small brands selling via Facebook/Zalo | **Print a static VietQR code and put it at the counter.** This is the single highest-ROI payment action available to a small Vietnamese business. |
| **E-wallet** | ví điện tử — MoMo, ZaloPay, ShopeePay, ViettelMoney, VNPAY | App-based ordering, online, promo-driven purchases | Accept at least MoMo. Wallet-funded promos are a real acquisition channel. |
| **COD** | thanh toán khi nhận hàng / COD | Shipped physical goods, especially outside the two big cities | Offer it if you ship product, and price the failure rate in. |

### 4.2 COD: the number to be careful with

Two different constructs get quoted as "COD share" and they are not the same thing.

- **Stated preference:** 35% of respondents say they prefer COD for online purchases in 2025 — still the
  leading single stated preference. `[verified]` (source: https://tgmresearch.com/vietnam-ecommerce-payment-shift-2025.html,
  published 2025-06-23, updated 2026-07-14, retrieved 2026-07-29). Methodology as stated on that page: online
  CAWI interviews, adults 18–54, nationally representative, N≈1,120 (the page itself gives N=1,120 in one place
  and N=1,142 in another — a reason to cite it carefully), weighted to regional demographics.
- **Transaction share:** "COD accounted for 67% of e-commerce transactions in 2024" `[search-level]` — I saw
  this only in a search summary attributed to a 2C2P/digitalinasia piece, and I could not verify the
  denominator. **Do not quote 67%.**

Also `[verified]` from the TGM page: BNPL usage rose from 17% (2022) to 49% (2025) among respondents, though
only 5% cite BNPL as a promotional appeal and 4% actually select it at checkout — a large usage-versus-selection
gap. Growth is described as strongest among 25–34s and women, without published percentages.

`[UNVERIFIED - needs a 2025/2026 transaction-level COD share for Vietnam from a logistics operator (GHN, Viettel
Post, J&T, SPX) or a platform]` The honest statement for a deck is: **COD remains the leading payment
*preference* for online purchases at 35% in 2025 `[verified]`, and remains materially more important in Vietnam
than in most Western markets — but the widely-circulated "67% of transactions" figure should not be used without
finding its primary source.**

Operational consequences of COD you must plan for `[structural]`:

- **Refusal-at-door risk.** COD orders can be refused on delivery. You eat the outbound and return shipping and
  the product may be unsellable. Perishables plus COD is a bad combination.
- **Cash-flow lag.** COD proceeds are remitted by the carrier on a settlement cycle, not on delivery.
- **The standard mitigation** is a partial deposit via VietQR plus COD for the balance, especially for made-to-
  order and high-ticket items. `[structural]`
- **Address quality.** Vietnamese addresses are often descriptive rather than canonical (alley/hẻm numbering,
  landmark references). Always capture a phone number and expect the driver to call. A checkout that makes the
  phone number optional will generate failed deliveries.

### 4.4 Tax withholding you will feel as a marketer

Three tax mechanics change marketing arithmetic in Vietnam. All three are things a Western-trained marketer does
not expect.

**(a) Marketplaces now withhold your tax at the transaction.** Decree **117/2025/NĐ-CP** on tax administration
for business on e-commerce and digital platforms, effective **2025-07-01**, requires platform operators
(Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop and other platforms with a payment function) to withhold and remit VAT and personal
income tax on behalf of household/individual sellers **at the moment the transaction is confirmed successful and
payment accepted**; sellers whose tax has been withheld and declared by the platform do not separately declare
those amounts. `[search-level]` (sources seen in summary:
https://thuvienphapluat.vn/van-ban/Thuong-mai/Nghi-dinh-117-2025-ND-CP-quan-ly-thue-hoat-dong-kinh-doanh-tren-nen-tang-thuong-mai-dien-tu-641913.aspx,
https://www.meinvoice.vn/tin-tuc/35149/nghi-dinh-117-2025-nd-cp-ve-thue-thuong-mai-dien-tu/, retrieved
2026-07-29). Consistent with the Shopee fee table's "VAT & PIT: 1.5%–4.5% depending on product category"
`[verified]` and with the "4.5% tax deducted before remitting" claims in the delivery-app reporting. `[UNVERIFIED
- needs the decree text read directly for the exact withholding rates by category]`

**Why it matters for marketing:** your marketplace net receipt is *after* commission, processing fee,
infrastructure fee, programme fees **and** withheld tax. A promotion priced off gross revenue will be
loss-making. Build your promo model on net remittance, and get one real settlement statement before you set a
discount depth.

**(b) Buying ads from Meta / Google / TikTok carries foreign-contractor tax.** `[verified]` (source:
https://gonnapass.com/thue-nha-thau-voi-facebook-google-tiktok/, published 2026-01-10, retrieved 2026-07-29):

| Period | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Before 2025-07-01 | combined 10%: VAT 5% + CIT 5% |
| From 2025-07-01 | **VAT increased to 10%** for services supplied by foreign suppliers without a permanent establishment in Vietnam via e-commerce/digital platforms |
| Legal basis cited | Law **48/2024/QH15**, Article 9(3) |
| Mechanics | differs by customer type — monthly-invoiced customers self-declare and remit; credit-card-paying customers have VAT calculated by the platform on the invoice |

**Why it matters:** your true cost per click is not the platform's billed amount. Budget for the tax on top, and
know which billing relationship you are in. What breaks if you ignore this: your CPA model is understated by a
material percentage and, if you are a registered company self-declaring, you accumulate an undeclared
withholding liability. Corroborating `[search-level]` detail: obligations arise under Circular 103/2014/TT-BTC
and Circular 80/2021/TT-BTC as amended by Circular 94/2025/TT-BTC, declared monthly or per occurrence.
`[UNVERIFIED - needs a tax adviser's confirmation for your specific entity type and billing arrangement]`

**(c) The endorser has a tax duty.** From 2026-01-01, advertising income must be declared and taxed by the
person doing the advertising `[verified]` (§8.2). Practical consequence: expect KOLs/KOCs to raise gross-up
questions, and expect invoices where previously you paid cash. Put tax responsibility in the contract
explicitly.

## 5. Pricing psychology and đồng formatting

### 5.1 The formatting rules, verified against the locale standard

This is the section where foreign-produced Vietnamese marketing most reliably outs itself as foreign. The rules
are objective and checkable.

Unicode CLDR data for the `vi` locale, `[verified]`:

| Property | Value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Decimal separator | `,` (comma) | `[verified]` (source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/unicode-org/cldr-json/main/cldr-json/cldr-numbers-full/main/vi/numbers.json, retrieved 2026-07-29) |
| Group (thousands) separator | `.` (full stop) | `[verified]` same source |
| Standard decimal pattern | `#,##0.###` (in CLDR pattern notation, where `,` and `.` are placeholders resolved by the symbols above) | `[verified]` same source |
| Standard currency pattern | `#,##0.00 ¤` — **currency symbol after the number, with a space** | `[verified]` same source |
| VND symbol | `₫` (U+20AB DONG SIGN) | `[verified]` (source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/unicode-org/cldr-json/main/cldr-json/cldr-numbers-full/main/vi/currencies.json, retrieved 2026-07-29) |
| VND narrow symbol | `₫` | `[verified]` same source |
| VND display name | `Đồng Việt Nam` | `[verified]` same source |

So: **seventy-nine thousand đồng is `79.000`, never `79,000`.** The comma-grouped form is an English-locale
artefact. Getting this wrong is the equivalent of writing "$79.000" on an American menu — it does not merely look
foreign, it is momentarily *unreadable*, because a Vietnamese reader parses `79,000` as seventy-nine-point-zero.

### 5.2 The five ways to write a price, and when to use each

`[structural]` — these are conventions of Vietnamese commercial practice. The CLDR standard covers only the
formal form; the rest are observed usage.

| Form | Example | Register | Use it for | Do not use it for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Retail đồng, lowercase suffix** | `79.000đ` | Everyday commercial. The default. | Menus, price tags, social posts, delivery-app listings, promo creative | Formal contracts |
| **Retail đồng, spaced suffix** | `79.000 đ` | Same register, slightly more typeset | Print menus, designed collateral | — |
| **CLDR-standard symbol** | `79.000 ₫` | Formal / software-generated | Invoices, checkout pages, receipts, anything a system renders | Handwritten signage (the ₫ glyph is rare in handwriting) |
| **"k" shorthand** | `79k` | Colloquial, young, informal | TikTok captions, Facebook comment replies, chat, informal promos, student-facing offers | Menus, invoices, anything a regulator or an older customer reads |
| **Formal three-letter code** | `79.000 VND` | Formal / financial / B2B / cross-border | B2B quotes, financial reporting, English-language materials, contracts | Consumer-facing retail — it reads as bureaucratic or foreign |

**The specific comparison in the brief — `79.000đ` vs `79,000 VND` — resolves like this:** `79.000đ` is correct
Vietnamese consumer retail. `79,000 VND` is wrong twice over for a consumer context: wrong group separator for
the `vi` locale `[verified]`, and a formal currency code where a colloquial suffix belongs. `79.000 VND` (with
the correct separator) is acceptable in a B2B or English-language document. If you must serve one string to both
audiences, use `79.000₫`.

Additional formatting conventions `[structural]`:

- **Vietnamese retail prices are almost always whole thousands.** `79.000đ`, not `79.500đ` unless there is a real
  reason; never `79.350đ`. Sub-thousand precision reads as a supermarket barcode, not a menu.
- **`triệu` (million) and `tỷ` (billion) are used in prose**, e.g. "gói tiệc từ 2,5 triệu đồng". Note the comma
  as decimal separator inside `2,5 triệu`.
- **Ranges use an en dash or "–" with the unit stated once**: `35.000 – 55.000đ`.
- **Discount phrasing**: `Giảm 20%`, `Giảm 20.000đ`, `Mua 1 tặng 1` (buy one get one), `Đồng giá 39.000đ`
  (everything one price), `Combo 2 người 149.000đ`. `Đồng giá` is a distinctively Vietnamese and very effective
  device — it removes decision cost across a whole menu section.
- **Do not write a struck-through "was" price you cannot substantiate.** See §8.3 — the 50% promotional cap and
  the general prohibition on misleading advertising both bite here.

### 5.3 Price-point anchors you can defend

The only price anchor in this dossier that is `[verified]`: **57.58% of the beverage segment sits in the
35.000đ-and-above spend bracket** (iPOS.vn × Nestlé Professional 2025 report, cited in §2). Read that as: in the
Vietnamese drinks market, 35.000đ+ is where the majority of the money is, not a premium position.

### 5.4 Pricing psychology: what is actually different here

`[structural]`, and I am deliberately not attaching invented percentages to any of these.

1. **Charm pricing (`.99` endings) is weak-to-absent.** With prices denominated in thousands, the Western
   `9.99` mechanism has no natural home. The Vietnamese analogue is the **round thousand** and, for a
   psychological step down, **dropping to the next 5.000 or 10.000 boundary** (`50.000đ` → `45.000đ`), not
   shaving đồng. `[UNVERIFIED - needs a Vietnamese price-ending experiment or a menu-price distribution study]`
   I could not find research on Vietnamese price-ending effects. Treat "round thousands dominate" as an
   observable convention you can verify yourself in 20 minutes of GrabFood browsing — and do verify it, because
   it is the basis of every price you will set.

2. **`Đồng giá` and combo framing do more work than percentage discounts.** A flat-price line (`Đồng giá
   39.000đ`) or a two-person combo removes comparison effort in a market with one outlet per ~310 people
   (§2). `[structural]`

3. **Delivery-app price and dine-in price are two different prices, and customers know it.** With 20–30%
   commission `[verified]`, the app price must carry the commission or your app orders are unprofitable. This is
   normal and broadly understood in Vietnam. What breaks if you keep one price: you subsidise your least
   loyal customers with margin taken from your most loyal ones.

4. **Free delivery beats a discount of the same value, and speed may beat both.** Rakuten Insight Q1 2025:
   speed prioritised by 47%, price by 26% `[verified]` (§1.7).

5. **Deposits are normal for seasonal and made-to-order goods.** Tết hampers, Trung thu mooncake boxes, catering
   and cakes are routinely sold on a deposit-plus-balance basis, which VietQR makes trivial (§4.1).
   `[structural]`

6. **Round-number thresholds for free shipping / free item are the standard basket-building lever on
   marketplaces**, e.g. `Freeship đơn từ 199.000đ`. `[structural]`

### 5.5 A pricing worked example (numbers illustrative)

`[illustrative]` — invented figures, for arithmetic only.

A shop sells a bowl at `65.000đ` dine-in. Food cost `22.000đ`. It wants the same contribution on GrabFood, which
takes 25% commission `[verified]` band midpoint, and it wants to run "Freeship" participation costing a further
5% of order value `[illustrative]`.

| Line | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dine-in price | | 65.000đ |
| Food cost | | 22.000đ |
| Dine-in contribution | 65.000 − 22.000 | **43.000đ** |
| Required app-price P | P × (1 − 0.25 − 0.05) − 22.000 = 43.000 | |
| Solve | (43.000 + 22.000) ÷ 0.70 | 92.857đ |
| Round to Vietnamese convention | up to nearest 1.000 | **93.000đ** |
| Sanity check | 93.000 × 0.70 − 22.000 | 43.100đ contribution ✓ |

Two lessons, both `[structural]`:

- **A 25%+5% take rate requires a ~43% price increase to hold contribution flat**, not a 30% increase. Marketers
  who add the commission percentage to the price instead of dividing by (1 − take rate) systematically
  under-price app menus and lose money on every order. This is the most common arithmetic error in Vietnamese
  small-F&B app pricing.
- **93.000đ against a 65.000đ dine-in price is a visible gap.** That is a positioning decision, not just an
  arithmetic one: either accept a smaller app contribution deliberately, or build an app-specific item (a
  differently-portioned or bundled product) so the two prices are not directly comparable. Also confirm your own
  platform contract permits a price differential — `[UNVERIFIED - needs the GrabFood/ShopeeFood Vietnam merchant
  terms on price parity]`. Some platforms restrict this; check before you publish.

## 6. Vietnamese language and tone for marketing copy

### 6.1 Non-negotiables

`[structural]`

1. **Diacritics, always, everywhere.** `Bún bò`, not `Bun bo`. Undiacriticised Vietnamese is legible but reads as
   lazy, cheap, or machine-generated, and creates real ambiguity (`bo`/`bò`/`bó`/`bọ`/`bố`). The only acceptable
   places to drop diacritics are domain names, hashtags where the platform mishandles them, and legacy SMS
   sender IDs. What breaks if you ignore this: your brand reads as a dropshipper.
2. **Test diacritic rendering in every destination.** Vietnamese uses combining and precomposed forms;
   `ề`, `ữ`, `ỡ`, `ặ` break in badly-configured fonts, CSV imports, SMS gateways and some ad-platform character
   counters. Always preview the live rendering, not the design file. What breaks: `Bún bò Hu?` on 40,000
   impressions.
3. **Vietnamese is verbose relative to English.** Expect Vietnamese copy to run longer than the English source
   for the same meaning — plan headline space accordingly. `[UNVERIFIED - needs a measured expansion ratio from
   a localisation vendor's Vietnamese language pair data]` I will not give you a percentage; measure your own
   strings.
4. **Never machine-translate a headline or a claim.** Body copy can survive MT plus editing. Headlines,
   taglines, product names and any health/quality claim cannot, both for tone and because §8 makes claim
   wording a legal matter.

### 6.2 Register, address and the particles that carry warmth

`[structural]` Vietnamese encodes politeness and relationship in pronouns and sentence-final particles. This is
where a translated-sounding ad becomes a Vietnamese-sounding one.

| Device | Form | Effect | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Second-person | **`bạn`** | Neutral, friendly, age-indeterminate "you" | The safe default for mass marketing copy |
| Second-person, respectful | **`quý khách`**, **`anh/chị`** | Formal / service register | Premium positioning, service comms, older audiences, B2B |
| Vocative | **`... ơi!`** (e.g. `Sài Gòn ơi!`, `Cả nhà ơi!`) | Calling out warmly; very Vietnamese, very effective in social copy | Facebook/TikTok captions, community posts |
| Softening particle | **`nhé`** (N), **`nha`** (S), **`nghen`** (S/Mekong) | Friendly nudge; softens an imperative into an invitation | CTAs: `Ghé quán mình nhé!` / `Ghé quán mình nha!` |
| Politeness particle | **`ạ`** | Respect toward the listener | Service replies, comms to older customers |
| Inclusive first person | **`mình`**, **`quán mình`**, **`nhà mình`** | "us / our shop / our household" — small-business warmth | Independent F&B. Enormously more effective than corporate `chúng tôi` for a single outlet |
| Corporate first person | **`chúng tôi`** | Institutional | Company statements, PR, B2B. Wrong for a noodle shop |

**Decision rule:** for an independent F&B outlet, default to `quán mình` + `bạn` + `nhé/nha`. For a packaged
consumer brand, default to `bạn` + brand name in third person. Reserve `quý khách` for service and premium.
What breaks if you get this wrong: a family bún bò shop that writes `chúng tôi` and `quý khách` sounds like a
bank, and loses precisely the intimacy that is its advantage against a chain.

### 6.3 Regional differences that actually change your copy

`[structural]` Vietnamese has three principal regional varieties — Northern (Bắc), Central (Trung), Southern
(Nam) — that are largely mutually intelligible but differ in pronunciation and, importantly for copy,
vocabulary. `[verified]` on the pronunciation contrasts (source:
https://vncomp.github.io/vi/docs/alphabet/regionaldif, retrieved 2026-07-29): Northern realises `d`, `gi`, `r`
as a /z/-type sound; Southern realises `d`, `gi`, `r`, `v` as a /j/-type sound and reduces `kh`/`qu` toward a
/hw/-type articulation; the same source states that Northern and Southern "normally just differ in pronunciation
and a few words here and there" while the Central variety "can almost feel like it has its own set of
vocabulary". `[search-level]`: the Southern merger of the `hỏi` and `ngã` tones, giving five tones in practice
rather than six, and `bắp cải` (N) vs `cải bắp` (S) for cabbage.

**Why this matters even though you are writing, not speaking:** because word choice is regional, and because
audio/video copy is spoken. Two concrete consequences:

1. **Choose a voice region deliberately for any video or audio.** A Northern voiceover selling a Southern dish in
   HCMC, or vice versa, is immediately noticeable. For a single-city business, use that city's variety.
   For national FMCG, Northern is the standard broadcast variety `[search-level]` — flag for re-check, this is
   widely repeated in language-teaching sources rather than in a citable style authority.
2. **Region-specific lexical choices exist for very ordinary marketing words.** `[UNVERIFIED - needs a
   citable dialect lexicon or a Vietnamese copy style guide]` **I am deliberately not reproducing a
   North/South word-pair table from memory.** I attempted to verify one and the sources I could read gave only
   pronunciation contrasts and a single vocabulary example (`bắp cải`/`cải bắp`). A wrong word-pair table would
   be actively harmful — it would put the wrong regional word into live copy. **How to close it properly:** have
   one native speaker from your target city read every piece of copy before it ships. That is a 10-minute task
   and it is strictly better than any table I could give you.

One legally-grounded lexical point I *can* nail down: **`khuyến mại` is the statutory term** for
sales-promotion activity — it is the term used in the promotion decree and its official commentary
(`hạn mức tối đa về giá trị hàng hóa, dịch vụ dùng để khuyến mại`, `chương trình khuyến mại tập trung`)
`[search-level]` (Decree 81/2018/NĐ-CP, sources at
https://vanban.vcci.com.vn/nghi-dinh-812018nd-cp-huong-dan-luat-thuong-mai-ve-hoat-dong-xuc-tien-thuong-mai and
https://lsvn.vn/sua-quy-dinh-han-muc-toi-da-ve-khuyen-mai-gia-tri-hang-hoa-dich-vu-1728689527-a148283.html,
retrieved 2026-07-29). `khuyến mãi` is the extremely common colloquial/commercial variant. **Rule: use
`khuyến mại` in anything filed, contractual or regulatory; either is fine in consumer creative, and
`khuyến mãi` is what customers say.**

### 6.4 English loanwords: when they help and when they hurt

`[structural]` Vietnamese marketing Vietnamese is heavily code-mixed, and this is normal, not sloppy. The
question is never "English or not" but "which English, for whom".

**English generally helps when:**

| Situation | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The term is already the Vietnamese term | `combo`, `topping`, `size`, `menu`, `voucher`, `freeship`, `deal`, `sale`, `review`, `livestream`, `order`, `check-in` | These are naturalised. Translating them out makes copy read as stilted or archaic. `Freeship` in particular is the standard word |
| Signalling modern/urban/premium positioning | `specialty coffee`, `cold brew`, `signature`, `craft` | Category conventions in specialty segments; the audience uses them |
| Platform-native vocabulary | `flash sale`, `livestream`, `giỏ hàng` alongside `add to cart` | Marketplace users expect them |
| Youth-targeted social copy | `chill`, `healthy`, `must-try` | Register match with 18–25 |

**English generally hurts when:**

| Situation | Why it hurts | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| It replaces a warm Vietnamese word with a cold English one | `Chúng tôi cung cấp premium dining experience` — corporate, distancing, and says nothing | `Quán mình nấu nước dùng từ 4 giờ sáng mỗi ngày` |
| The audience is older, rural, or lower-income | Comprehension risk plus a status signal that excludes | Full Vietnamese |
| It carries a *claim* | Regulated wording must be intelligible and substantiable; an English superlative is still a superlative (§8.3) | Vietnamese, substantiated |
| It is a whole English headline in a mass-market ad | Reduces reach for no benefit | Vietnamese headline, English only in the product descriptor |
| It signals foreignness the brand cannot back | A local F&B brand with all-English copy invites a "why?" | Vietnamese-first, English accents |
| Health/food-safety information | Must be understood; regulated (§8.5) | Vietnamese, exactly as prescribed |

**Decision rule:** a loanword is acceptable when your target customer would use it *speaking to a friend*. If
they would not, it is decoration and it costs you comprehension. What breaks if you over-index on English: you
sound like a foreign brand's translated deck, which is exactly the credibility position a small local F&B brand
cannot afford.

### 6.5 Copy patterns that work in Vietnamese F&B

`[structural]`, with plain-Vietnamese examples you can adapt. These are craft patterns, not researched claims.

| Pattern | Vietnamese example | Why it works here |
|---|---|---|
| Process/provenance proof | `Nước dùng nấu 6 tiếng từ xương bò tươi, không bột ngọt` | Answers the food-safety anxiety that 54.45% of consumers demonstrably act on `[verified]` §2 |
| Named person | `Chị Ba nấu bún bò Huế 18 năm` | Trust attaches to people, not brands, at this scale |
| Explicit price transparency | `Bún bò đặc biệt 65.000đ — không phụ thu` | Pre-empts the fear of hidden charges |
| Neighbourhood specificity | `Ngay hẻm 42 Nguyễn Văn Đậu, gần chợ Bà Chiểu` | Local search is by landmark, not by address |
| Time-bound honesty | `Mở 6h–10h sáng, hết là dọn` | Scarcity that is true. "Hết là dọn" (we close when it's gone) is a real and highly credible Vietnamese device |
| Community call | `Cả nhà ơi, hôm nay quán mình có…` | Matches the group-based discovery reality (§1.3) |
| Soft CTA | `Ghé quán mình nha!` / `Đặt trước qua Zalo 09xx nhé` | Invitation, not command |

**Anti-patterns:** superlatives you cannot document (`ngon nhất`, `số 1`) — these are specifically penalised,
see §8.3; health claims on ordinary food (`giúp giảm cân`, `tốt cho tim mạch`) — see §8.5; fake urgency
(`chỉ còn 2 suất!` when untrue); and stock photography of food that is not your food.

## 7. Seasonality and the occasion calendar

### 7.1 Statutory public holidays — the fixed skeleton

`[verified]` Article 112(1) of the Labour Code 2019 grants **11 paid public holiday days** (source:
https://hethongphapluat.com/bo-luat-lao-dong-2019/dieu-112, retrieved 2026-07-29), quoted:

> "Tết Dương lịch: 01 ngày; Tết Âm lịch: 05 ngày; Ngày Chiến thắng: 01 ngày; Ngày Quốc tế lao động: 01 ngày;
> Quốc khánh: 02 ngày; Ngày Giỗ Tổ Hùng Vương: 01 ngày."

| Holiday | Date | Days | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tết Dương lịch (Gregorian New Year) | 1 January | 1 | |
| **Tết Âm lịch / Tết Nguyên Đán (Lunar New Year)** | lunar, moves | **5** | The dominant commercial event of the year |
| Ngày Chiến thắng (Reunification Day) | 30 April | 1 | Runs into 1 May as a bloc |
| Ngày Quốc tế Lao động (Labour Day) | 1 May | 1 | |
| Quốc khánh (National Day) | 2 September + one adjacent day | 2 | |
| Giỗ Tổ Hùng Vương (Hùng Kings) | 10th day of 3rd lunar month | 1 | |

Two structural rules that matter for scheduling `[search-level]`, from the same body of sources: the Prime
Minister decides the specific Tết and National Day arrangement each year based on circumstances, and if a weekly
rest day coincides with a public holiday the employee takes the rest day on the next working day — which is why
Tết in practice becomes a **7-to-9 day continuous shutdown** rather than 5. Also `[search-level]`: foreign
employees working in Vietnam get one extra day for their own national traditional new year and one for their own
national day.

**Operational consequence:** Vietnam does not have a "long weekend" culture spread through the year; it has a
small number of very large blocs. Plan staffing, inventory, supplier lead times and ad flighting around blocs,
not around weekends.

2026-07-29. **Re-check against the Prime Minister's official holiday decision for 2027, which is normally issued

`[structural]` The Tết commercial cycle, in phases. Anchor everything to **D = mùng 1**.

| Phase | Window | What happens | What a small F&B / consumer brand should be doing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gifting build-up** | D−60 to D−21 | Corporate and personal gift buying: hampers (`giỏ quà Tết`), premium boxes, alcohol, tea, confectionery, specialty coffee | This is when a packaged brand makes its year. Gift SKUs, deposit-based pre-orders (VietQR §4.1), corporate outreach. Start the B2B conversation at D−60, not D−20 |
| **Peak retail** | D−21 to D−3 | Household stocking, cleaning, new clothes, flowers, decoration; delivery volumes spike; couriers hit capacity | Cut-off dates published clearly. | **The shutdown** | D−2 to D+3 approx | Cities empty as workers return to home provinces; most independent F&B closes; delivery riders scarce; ad CPMs behave abnormally | Decide *early* and *publicly* whether you close. | **Return / reopening** | D+3 to D+10 | Staggered return; "mở hàng" (first sale of the year, culturally significant); appetite for eating out returns fast | A reopening announcement on Facebook + Zalo broadcast is one of the highest-response messages you will send all year |

- **Staff will leave and may not come back.** Post-Tết labour churn is the single biggest operational risk to a

- **Do not run acquisition spend into the shutdown.** You are paying to reach people who are travelling and whose
  local shops are shut.
- **Supplier lead times extend for weeks either side.** Confirm ingredient availability before you promote a

- **Ad platform auction behaviour around Tết is not something I have verified.** `[UNVERIFIED - needs your own
  historical CPM/CPC data or a Meta/TikTok Vietnam seasonality report]` Do not assume either "cheap because

  because discounting now runs year-round via double-digit days, monthly super-sales and livestream sales
  `[search-level]` (sources seen in summary: https://vtv.vn/black-friday-2025-sale-sap-san-van-vang-khach-mua-100251128123836092.htm
  — headlined "sale sập sàn" but "vẫn vắng khách mua" i.e. heavy discounts, few buyers; and
  https://www.24h.com.vn/thi-truong-tieu-dung/bien-nguoi-ky-luc-di-san-sale-black-friday-nam-nay-lai-la-mua-khuyen-mai-keo-kiet-nhat-c52a1716629.html
  — describing record crowds but the "stingiest" discount season, retrieved 2026-07-29). **These two
  characterisations conflict** (empty stores vs record crowds), which is itself informative: Black Friday in
  Vietnam is inconsistent and channel-dependent.

**Decision rule:** treat Black Friday as an *offline-retail and imported-brand* moment and as a tail of the 12.12
chain online. For a small F&B business it is close to irrelevant — it has no food-occasion logic. Put your
year-end effort into 20/11, Giáng sinh atmosphere, and the Tết gifting build-up, in that order.

### 7.5 Trung thu / mooncake mechanics

`[structural]` `[search-level]` on the date: Tết Trung thu 2026 falls on **Friday 25 September 2026** (15th day
of the 8th lunar month) — seen in multiple Vietnamese calendar pages
(e.g. https://quantrimang.com/cuoc-song/trung-thu-la-ngay-bao-nhieu-183274, retrieved 2026-07-29), verify against
a printed lịch vạn niên before printing anything.

`[UNVERIFIED - needs a citable Vietnamese mooncake-market report (size, corporate vs retail split, typical
selling-window start) or a manufacturer's published season dates]` What I can state structurally: mooncakes are
sold as **boxed gift sets** with a large corporate-gifting component; the selling window opens weeks before the
festival day and collapses immediately after it, leaving unsold stock nearly worthless. This makes it a
**pre-order and deposit business**, not a stock-and-hope business. If you make a seasonal gift product:

1. Open pre-orders early and take deposits by VietQR.
2. Sell corporate volume before retail — one order of 200 boxes de-risks the season.
3. Set a hard order cut-off and publish it.
4. Plan the day-after markdown before the season starts, and plan it as a *loyalty* gesture (free box with a
   meal, staff gifting) rather than a public discount that teaches customers to wait.

### 8.2 The 2026 change that most affects you: endorsers now have personal duties

`[verified]` (source: https://luatvietnam.vn/linh-vuc-khac/kols-quang-cao-san-pham-can-luu-y-04-quy-dinh-moi-tu-01-01-2026-883-106335-article.html,
published 2026-01-09, retrieved 2026-07-29). From **1 January 2026**, under Law 75/2025/QH15, a person who
advertises, recommends or endorses a product ("người chuyển tải sản phẩm quảng cáo") must:

| # | Duty | Cited provision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ensure truthfulness and comply with consumer protection** — convey accurate product information, no exaggeration, no misleading claims about features or benefits | Article 15a |
| 2 | **Fulfil tax obligations** on advertising income | Article 15a |
| 3 | **Provide documentation on request** — contracts, product materials, advertising content — to competent authorities during inspection | Article 15a |
| 4 | **Disclose the advertising clearly** — posts must carry a clear indicator (text, symbol, image or audio) distinguishing advertising from ordinary content, especially on social media | Article 23(2)–(3) |

Additional duties in the same `[verified]` source:

- **Verify product credibility before promoting**; avoid endorsing products not personally tested.
- **Do not partner with parties who violate advertising law.**
- **Respond to removal requests within 24 hours** where content violates the regulations.

Both of those last two bullets have since been read at primary-source level, and the secondary source
under-described them. The 24-hour duty is Nghị định 342/2025 Điều 18.2 and it binds the advertiser, the
ad-service business, the publisher and the conveyor alike rather than only an endorser; the request
counts when it arrives electronically, and a miss brings technical blocking by two ministries under
Điều 18.3. The partnering rule is Nghị định 87/2026 Điều 56.3.c, and it is triggered by a public
announcement rather than by what you knew. Read `data/vn-advertising-law.csv` and
`references/pr-communications.md` for both, not this table.

The fine bands the secondary source could not supply are now in that table, from Nghị định 87/2026:
Điều 51 for endorser breaches, Điều 56 for online placement and Điều 57 for print.

Corroborating `[search-level]`: the amended law is characterised as introducing a **post-verification
("hậu kiểm") principle** — shifting from pre-publication licensing to monitoring and enforcement after
publication — and as strengthening consumer remedies including complaint, compensation claims and referral for
criminal prosecution in serious cases. Also `[search-level]`: a handbook ("cẩm nang") for KOLs and KOCs was
reported as planned for 2026 (https://plo.vn/luat-quang-cao-2025-se-co-cam-nang-cho-kol-koc-trong-nam-2026-post880999.html)
— worth watching for, because it will be the practical compliance reference.

**What to put in every influencer contract from now on, as a direct consequence:**

1. A representation that the creator will label the content as advertising in the manner required by Article 23.
2. A representation that the creator has used or genuinely understands the product, with the mechanism recorded
   (visit date, order receipt, sample dispatch note).
3. Your obligation to supply substantiation documents, and their obligation to retain the brief, contract and
   final assets for inspection.
4. A 24-hour takedown/edit cooperation clause.
5. Tax: who declares, who bears, and whether the fee is gross or net.
6. Your right to review claims wording before publication — because their claim becomes your problem too.

What breaks if you ignore this: your creator publishes an unlabelled post with an unsupported health or
superlative claim, and you have no contractual basis to compel a fix, no substantiation file, and a documented
paper trail showing you commissioned it.

### 8.3 Prohibited claim wording — the superlative rule

`[search-level]`, and this is the single most practically-relevant claim rule for a small F&B brand:
advertising that uses **"nhất", "duy nhất", "tốt nhất", "số một"** or words of equivalent meaning **without
lawful documentary evidence** is a violation, penalised under Decree 38/2021/NĐ-CP at **10,000,000–20,000,000đ
for an individual and 20,000,000–40,000,000đ for an organisation** (sources seen in search summary:
https://vanban.vcci.com.vn/nghi-dinh-382021nd-cp-quy-dinh-xu-phat-vi-pham-hanh-chinh-trong-linh-vuc-van-hoa-va-quang-cao
and https://vcci.com.vn/legal-document/nghi-dinh-382021nd-cp-quy-dinh-xu-phat-vi-pham-hanh-chinh-trong-linh-vuc-van-hoa-va-quang-cao,
retrieved 2026-07-29). **Re-check the current band against the decree text as amended — penalty levels are
routinely revised.** `[UNVERIFIED - needs the decree article number and the current amended fine band]`

**This is why §6.5 lists `ngon nhất` and `số 1` as anti-patterns.** The practical translation:

| Do not write | Write instead | Why the substitute is safe |
|---|---|---|
| `Bún bò ngon nhất Sài Gòn` | `Bún bò đậm vị Huế — nấu theo công thức gia đình 3 đời` | Specific, descriptive, attributable to you |
| `Cà phê số 1 Việt Nam` | `Cà phê rang mộc, 100% Arabica Cầu Đất` | A factual product characteristic |
| `Chất lượng tốt nhất` | `Nguyên liệu nhập từ [supplier], có chứng nhận [cert]` | Documentable |
| `Duy nhất tại Việt Nam` | (only if you hold documentation proving it) | The rule permits it *with* lawful evidence |
| `Giá rẻ nhất thị trường` | `Đồng giá 39.000đ` | A statement of your own price, not a comparison |

`[UNVERIFIED - needs the amended Article 8 list read in full]` I did not read the amended Article 8 prohibited-acts
list. Other categories I know exist in the Vietnamese framework but did not verify in this pass, and which you
must check if relevant: comparative advertising against a named competitor; advertising of tobacco and of
alcohol at or above certain strengths (a search summary referenced fines up to 70,000,000đ for individuals
advertising tobacco or alcohol at ≥15 degrees under Decree 38/2021 `[search-level]`); advertising that offends
national dignity, culture or beliefs; advertising to or using children. **If your product is alcoholic, stop and
get advice before writing anything.**

### 8.4 Personal data and direct marketing from 2026

`[search-level]` under Law 91/2025/QH15, in force 1 January 2026:

- Data subjects have rights to know, to consent, to access, to correct and to **request deletion**, and a right
  to **request that advertising communications cease**.
- An organisation providing advertising services may only use customer personal data that was **transferred to it
  by a data controller under an agreement, or collected through its own business operations**.
- Processing customer personal data for advertising requires **consent**, and that consent must be informed as to
  the **content, method, form and frequency** of the product introductions, and the customer must be given a
  **means to refuse** to receive advertising.
- Use of personal data for advertising must also comply with anti-spam rules for messages, email and calls, and
  with advertising law.
- Advertising service providers must provide a mechanism to stop advertising on request.

Sources seen in search summaries only: the statute pages listed in §8.1 plus
https://cafef.vn/tu-1-1-2026-nguoi-dan-co-quyen-yeu-cau-xoa-du-lieu-tu-choi-quang-cao-188251228142412298.chn
and https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=65a59086-643c-467f-bb40-2cf465eaca13, retrieved 2026-07-29.
`[UNVERIFIED - needs the statute text read directly, and the implementing decree, for the exact consent
requirements and any SME exemptions or transition period]`

**Minimum practical compliance posture for a small brand, given the above:**

1. **Capture consent at the point of phone-number collection**, in Vietnamese, stating what you will send, how
   often, and how to stop. A line on the loyalty card and a checkbox on the order form.
2. **Keep a record of when and how each consent was given.** A spreadsheet with source, date and wording is
   better than nothing and is what an inspector will ask for.
3. **Honour opt-out immediately** and keep a suppression list. Never re-add a number from an old export.
4. **State the frequency and stick to it.** The consent requirement is frequency-specific per the summaries
   above.
5. **Do not buy phone-number lists.** This was always bad practice; from 2026 it has a direct legal problem,
   since you cannot show a lawful basis.
6. **Zalo's own rules already enforce much of this** (§1.4: OA can only message users who follow or have
   interacted). Using Zalo OA properly gets you most of the way; using scraped numbers via ZNS does not.

### 8.5 Food, health and functional-food claims

`[search-level]` under Decree 15/2018/NĐ-CP and related rules:

- **Health-supplement foods (thực phẩm bảo vệ sức khỏe) must carry the disclaimer** *"Thực phẩm này không phải là
  thuốc và không có tác dụng thay thế thuốc chữa bệnh"* ("This product is not a medicine and does not replace
  curative medicine"), in clear type with contrasting colour against the background.
- For broadcast advertising **shorter than 15 seconds**, the disclaimer need not be *read aloud* but must still be
  *displayed*.
- Failure to include, mis-state, fail to read clearly, or fail to display the disclaimer attracts a fine of
  **10,000,000–20,000,000đ**.
- **Advertising content for health-supplement foods must be registered in advance** and a confirmation certificate
  (Giấy xác nhận nội dung quảng cáo) obtained; the receiving authority is the **Cục An toàn thực phẩm** (Vietnam
  Food Administration) under the Ministry of Health.

Sources seen in search summaries: https://vfa.gov.vn/thu-tuc-hanh-chinh/dang-ky-xac-nhan-noi-dung-quang-cao-doi-voi-thuc-pham-bao-ve-suc-khoe.html
(the Food Administration's own procedure page), https://dichvucong.gov.vn/p/home/dvc-chi-tiet-thu-tuc-hanh-chinh.html?ma_thu_tuc=1.006424&id_bo_nganh=4881
(the national public-service portal entry), and https://thuvienphapluat.vn/phap-luat/doanh-nghiep-quang-cao-thuc-pham-bao-ve-suc-khoe-khong-co-khuyen-cao-co-duoc-khong-doanh-nghiep-qua-263035-146093.html,
all retrieved 2026-07-29. `[UNVERIFIED - needs the vfa.gov.vn procedure page and Decree 15/2018 Article 27 read
directly for the full list of food categories requiring pre-registration, and the current fee and turnaround]`

**The line that matters for ordinary F&B.** The registration and disclaimer regime attaches to *health-supplement
and certain other regulated food categories* — not to a bowl of noodles. **But the moment your marketing crosses
from describing food into promising a health effect, you have arguably repositioned your product into a regulated
category and you have made a claim you must substantiate.** So:

| Safe (descriptive) | Dangerous (health claim) |
|---|---|
| `Không bột ngọt` (no MSG) — a factual composition statement about your own product | `Giúp giảm cân` (helps you lose weight) |
| `Ít đường` — if true and you can show the recipe | `Tốt cho tim mạch` (good for the heart) |
| `Nguyên liệu tươi, nhập mỗi sáng` | `Tăng cường miễn dịch` (boosts immunity) |
| `Nấu bằng nước dừa tươi` | `Chữa đau dạ dày` (cures stomach pain) |
| `Có chứng nhận cơ sở đủ điều kiện an toàn thực phẩm` — if you hold it | `Detox`, `thanh lọc cơ thể` used as an efficacy promise |

**Rule: describe the food, never the outcome in the body.** What breaks if you ignore it: at minimum a takedown
and a fine; at worst you have marketed an unregistered health product, which is a materially more serious
category of violation, and under §8.2 your KOC carries exposure alongside you.

Also relevant and `[UNVERIFIED - needs confirmation of the applicable instruments]`: an F&B business selling
prepared food generally needs a food-safety eligibility certificate (Giấy chứng nhận cơ sở đủ điều kiện an toàn
thực phẩm) or a qualifying exemption, and packaged food carries Vietnamese labelling obligations (I did not verify
the labelling decree in this pass). If you package and sell product, get both checked. Given that 54.45% of
consumers demonstrably changed purchase behaviour after a food-safety incident `[verified]` (§2), your
certificates are marketing assets — photograph them and display them.

### 8.6 Social platform identity rules that affect livestream selling

`[search-level]` under Decree 147/2024/NĐ-CP, effective 25/12/2024:

- Social networks must **verify users by phone number or personal identification number**, and only verified
  accounts may post, comment or livestream.
- Where a user **livestreams for commercial purposes**, the platform must verify the account by **personal
  identification number** under the electronic identification and authentication rules.
- Per point (e), clause 3, Article 23, **foreign cross-border providers with over 100,000 visits per month from
  Vietnam** must verify user accounts via a Vietnamese mobile number.

Sources seen in search summaries: https://mst.gov.vn/xac-thuc-tai-khoan-mang-xa-hoi-bang-so-dien-thoai-cu-hich-moi-tu-nghi-dinh-147-2024-nd-cp-197241227105213649.htm
and https://thoibaotaichinhvietnam.vn/nhung-diem-moi-ve-su-dung-mang-xa-hoi-tu-25122024-167198.html, retrieved

**Practical consequence:** if you plan to sell by livestream — your own or a KOS's — the person on camera must be

### 9.1 First 30 days for a new small F&B brand in Vietnam

- [ ] Facebook Page created, complete: exact address with landmark, opening hours, phone, menu with prices in

- [ ] Copy read aloud by one native speaker from your target city (§6.3).
- [ ] Food-safety certificate obtained and photographed for use as content (§8.5).
- [ ] A single measurement mechanism in place — per-creator code word or QR — before booking any KOC (§3.4).

### 9.2 Pre-publication claim check (run on every asset)

1. Any superlative — `nhất`, `số 1`, `duy nhất`, `tốt nhất`, or English equivalents? If yes: delete, or attach
   documentary evidence to the file. (§8.3)
2. Any health outcome promised? If yes: delete or reduce to a composition fact. (§8.5)
3. Any comparison to a named competitor? Get advice.
4. Any struck-through "was" price? Can you substantiate the prior price and is the discount within the
   promotion cap? (§8.3, §9.3)
5. Is this an influencer post? Is it labelled as advertising per Article 23? (§8.2)
6. Are all prices formatted `xx.000đ`? (§5.1)
7. Do all diacritics render correctly in the live preview, not the design file? (§6.1)
8. Is there a real photograph of your actual product, not stock? (§6.5)
9. If it collects a phone number: is there consent wording stating content, method, frequency and how to opt
   out? (§8.4)
10. If it is a Zalo broadcast: is the send time within 06:00–19:59? (§1.4)

### 9.3 Promotion depth check

`[search-level]` under Decree 81/2018/NĐ-CP as amended by Decree 128/2024/NĐ-CP (sources §6.3, §8.1):

- The **maximum reduction is 50%** of the value of the promoted goods/services immediately before the promotion
  period, and the total value of goods/services used for promotion in a programme may not exceed 50% of the total
  value of the goods/services being promoted, with exceptions for certain promotion forms.
- The cap rises to **100%** for **concentrated promotion programmes** ("chương trình khuyến mại tập trung":
  promotional hours, days, weeks, months or seasons) and for promotions within programmes decided by the Prime
  Minister.

`[UNVERIFIED - needs Decree 81/2018 Articles 6-10 and Decree 128/2024 read directly for the exact article
numbers, the notification/registration thresholds, and which promotion forms are exempt]` My fetch of the

**Practical rule:** a routine "Giảm 50%" is at the legal ceiling; anything deeper needs to sit inside a
recognised concentrated promotion programme. Also note the **notification/registration obligation** — many
