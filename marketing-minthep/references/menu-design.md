# Menu Design and Engineering

Use this reference for printed menus, menu boards, delivery listings, QR menus, restaurant landing pages, and food-brand concept directions. Do not begin with visual style; begin with item economics, customer decision order, operational truth, and the ordering environment. The detailed evidence audit and worked Vietnamese example live in `dossiers/menu-design-and-engineering.md`.

## Required inputs

- Item name, category, price, portion, ingredients, allergens/dietary markers, and availability.
- Plate cost and units sold when menu engineering is requested.
- Channel: table menu, counter board, delivery app, QR/mobile, poster, or social post.
- Brand position, service speed, daypart, audience, language, and photography assets.
- Printing size/material or digital viewport.

Never invent prices, ingredients, provenance, dietary claims, awards, spice levels, or availability.

## Engineering before decoration

Use contribution margin in currency, not margin percentage:

```text
contribution margin = selling price - plate cost
menu mix % = item units / category units * 100
popularity threshold = (1 / number of comparable items) * 0.70
weighted average CM = sum(item CM * item units) / total category units
```

Run separate analyses for mains, add-ons, drinks, desserts, and channel-only items. Mixing them can silently change classifications. Label the method and threshold. Treat Stars, Plowhorses, Puzzles, and Dogs as hypotheses for pricing, placement, naming, bundling, or removal; protect dietary-gate items and operational necessities.

## Information architecture

Design for sequential scanning, not the disproven restaurant `golden triangle` myth.

1. Orient: restaurant name, service proposition, language, ordering instructions.
2. Decide: categories in natural meal order; hero or recommended item near the start of its category.
3. Compare: consistent item name, short sensory/provenance descriptor, portion, and price alignment.
4. Complete the basket: add-ons, beverages, combos, and desserts near the point where they become relevant.
5. Reassure: spice, dietary, allergen, service charge, availability, and contact details.

Keep categories distinct through spacing and type hierarchy. Do not use boxes, photos, badges, color, icons, and oversized type on the same item. One emphasis device per priority is usually enough.

## Three direction families

### Modern editorial

- Strong typographic hierarchy, generous spacing, limited photography, asymmetrical but disciplined grid.
- Best for focused menus, premium casual concepts, or a signature dish.
- Risk: excessive whitespace can reduce scannability and item density on small formats.

### Warm vernacular

- Local materials, warm neutrals, hand-made cues, documentary food photography, culturally specific language.
- Best when place, craft, family recipe, or street-food authenticity is central.
- Risk: decorative nostalgia can become illegible or stereotyped.

### Fast-service modular

- Large price and item name, clear category blocks, combo logic, high-distance readability, minimal copy.
- Best for counter boards, kiosks, food courts, delivery thumbnails, and high-throughput ordering.
- Risk: visual noise and promotion overload.

For each direction deliver: rationale, wireframe, type hierarchy, palette roles, photo plan, component rules, print/digital specs, and a prompt contract. Do not present style adjectives without these decisions.

## Food image plan

For a dish such as bun bo, offer a coherent set rather than one generic hero:

- Overhead menu-recognition image with bowl geometry and toppings clear.
- Three-quarter appetite hero with broth depth, steam, sheen, and edible entry point.
- Macro proof image for noodles, meat, herbs, broth, or preparation.
- Scale/context image with hand, tableware, or place setting.
- Delivery/listing cutout with clean silhouette and truthful portion.

Preserve real serving size, ingredient truth, bowl contact, heat behavior, and garnish logic. Avoid ingredient explosions, impossible steam, duplicated toppings, and over-saturated reds/greens.

## Wireframe contract

```text
CANVAS / VIEWPORT
READING DISTANCE AND ORDERING CONTEXT
HEADER / ORIENTATION
CATEGORY ORDER
ITEM ROW: name -> descriptor -> portion/marker -> price
HERO EMPHASIS DEVICE
ADD-ON / COMBO LOCATION
FOOTER / LEGAL / ALLERGEN / SERVICE INFORMATION
PHOTO SLOTS AND CROP
TYPE SIZES, SPACING, GRID, COLOR ROLES
```

## Verification

- Recalculate every price and contribution margin from source data.
- Read at final physical size or actual phone viewport.
- Check contrast, Vietnamese diacritics, currency formatting, and line wrapping.
- Confirm all food photos match the served product.
- Export print work with the printer's bleed, profile, and finishing requirements; verify live platform requirements for delivery apps.
