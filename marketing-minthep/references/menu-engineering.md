# Menu Engineering

For a dish photo or restaurant brief, propose options before designing: `classic/local`, `modern editorial`, and `high-volume readable`. Recommend one based on audience, venue, price point, print/digital surface, and service speed.

Load `menu-design.md` for the full menu research, information architecture, typography, print, accessibility, and production method. This file is the short decision contract used by the pipeline registry.

## Content model

Capture dish name, pronunciation if needed, ingredients confirmed, allergens, portion, price, modifiers, dietary labels, availability, and one truthful sensory description. Never infer ingredients or prices from a photo.

## Layout brief

Define paper/screen size, grid, section hierarchy, type scale, price alignment, image ratio, whitespace, contrast, bilingual order, and a text-safe area. Produce a low-fidelity wireframe before a visual prompt. Keep each item scannable in under two seconds and test the longest real item name.

## Visual direction

For food images specify hero angle (usually 30-45 degrees or overhead), lens feel, steam/texture only when truthful, controlled highlights, contact shadow, garnish limits, background color, and crop variants. Use the same visual grammar across the menu, social post, and delivery thumbnail.

## Outputs

Return three option cards, a recommended direction with rationale, a wireframe, copy pack, image prompt(s), print/digital specs, and a QA checklist for price, allergens, legibility, bleed, and export.
