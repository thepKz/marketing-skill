## Strategy before calendar

Every content item must connect:

```text
audience state -> question/tension -> useful promise -> evidence -> format -> channel -> next action
```

Do not begin with a list of generic topics. Define the change in understanding or behavior first.

## Pillar construction

Create three to five pillars. Each pillar needs:

- Audience problem or ambition.
- Product or brand authority to speak.
- Repeatable evidence source.
- Funnel role: discover, trust, convert, or retain.
- Distinctive point of view.
- Boundaries: what the pillar will not cover.

Useful pillar roles:

1. **Problem language**: help the audience name and diagnose the issue.
2. **Mechanism education**: explain how the solution works.
3. **Proof in practice**: demonstrate outcomes, process, or customer evidence.
4. **Decision support**: comparisons, objections, costs, risks, and selection criteria.
5. **Use and expansion**: onboarding, workflows, advanced use, and retention.

## Content brief

For each asset define:

- Working title and search/social hook.
- Target audience state and single reader question.
- One-sentence takeaway.
- Original angle and saturated angle to reject.
- Evidence, examples, sources, and product truth required.
- Structure or beat sheet.
- Primary CTA and natural next asset.
- Channel, format, length, owner, due date, and reuse potential.
- Claim and rights risks.

For SEO work, verify live search intent and current results. Do not infer keyword volume or ranking difficulty without data.

## Repurposing graph

Repurpose the idea, not the file:

- Research or customer insight -> long-form source asset.
- Source asset -> social point-of-view posts, carousel, short video script, email, sales snippet.
- Proof fragment -> ad or landing-page module.
- Audience question -> FAQ, comparison, onboarding, or support content.

Adapt hook, pacing, evidence density, CTA, and visual grammar for each channel. Do not paste the same copy everywhere.

## Editorial cadence

Prefer a sustainable operating loop:

1. Capture audience questions and product evidence.
2. Prioritize by strategic value, demand signal, and production cost.
3. Brief and produce.
4. Review facts, claims, voice, and channel fit.
5. Publish only when explicitly authorized.
6. Distribute and repurpose.
7. Measure against the intended behavior.
8. Refresh, consolidate, or retire based on evidence.

## Quality gate

Reject content that:

- Could be published by any competitor after changing the logo.
- Makes a promise without mechanism or evidence.
- Uses a trend as the entire idea.
- Adds length without increasing clarity or value.
- Hides the product relationship until an arbitrary final CTA.
- Invents statistics, quotations, customer stories, or search data.
- Optimizes a headline for clicks while violating the content promise.
