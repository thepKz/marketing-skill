# Marketing Canon

The decision layer above every other reference: what the best practitioners concluded after
careers of spending other people's money, compressed into rules that pick a side. Each rule names
who it distills, what grade of evidence backs it, and the condition under which it fails — a rule
quoted without its failure condition is decoration, not judgment. Practitioner judgment is a
legitimate input for *decisions*; it is banned as public-facing *proof*. "Ogilvy said" never
appears in an ad, a deck, or a client-facing claim — it appears here, once, so the run can decide
faster.

## The order of work is diagnosis, strategy, tactics

Ritson's sequence, Kotler's STP underneath it: understand the market, choose who and what, only
then choose how. Almost every brief arrives as a tactic — "làm cái poster", "viết caption" — with
the diagnosis and strategy silently assumed. The rule is not to force the client back through the
sequence; it is to run the sequence in your own head before executing the tactic, which takes a
minute: who is this for, what one thing must they believe, what would they use instead. A `direct`
run still gets that minute. **Fails when:** the client has already run the sequence and locked the
answer — re-deriving it then is second-guessing, and the locked content wins (`design-direction.md`).

## Sell the hole, not the drill

Levitt: nobody wants a quarter-inch drill; they want a quarter-inch hole — and behind the hole, a
shelf, and behind the shelf, a tidy home. The product is what it does for the buyer, stated at the
level the buyer actually shops at. Drucker sits behind this: the aim of marketing is to know the
customer so well the product sells itself, which makes product truth the strongest marketing input
this skill ever receives — stronger than any creative decision downstream. **Fails when:** the
buyer is an expert shopping on specifications (B2B procurement, developers, pharmacists); experts
buy drills and resent being sold holes. Check the audience's expertise before choosing the level.

## Grow by penetration first

Sharp and Ehrenberg-Bass: brands grow mainly by acquiring more buyers, mostly light ones, not by
deepening loyalty — double jeopardy says small brands lose on both counts anyway. The empirical
grades and their honest limits live in `how-companies-market.md`; this file only carries the
decision: when a plan proposes loyalty mechanics while penetration is flat, redirect the budget
toward reach and new-buyer acquisition, and treat the loyalty line as retention hygiene, not
growth. **Fails when:** the business is subscription or repeat-purchase with material churn — there
the bucket math (`lifecycle-retention.md`, `pricing-and-offers.md`) dominates and retention is the
growth lever; and in two-person Vietnamese businesses where "loyalty" means the Zalo group that
produces most of revenue (`vietnam-operating-reality.md`).

## Distinctive before different — and when different wins

Two schools claim this ground. Reeves and the USP tradition: win by a unique claim the competitor
cannot make. Sharp and Romaniuk: buyers barely notice claims; win by distinctive assets — colour,
shape, character, sound — that make the brand findable in memory and on shelf. The resolution is
attention, not allegiance to a school: in low-involvement, habit-bought categories (drinks, snacks,
household), distinctiveness carries nearly everything and differentiation claims evaporate; in
considered, compared purchases (software, courses, services, equipment), a true difference stated
plainly earns the sale and distinctiveness only earns the shortlist. Both schools agree on the
floor: generic is death. `data/slop-tells.csv` measures the category-reflex failure; `identity-design.md`
holds the asset register. **Fails when:** the "difference" is unverifiable or trivial — then it is
neither distinctive nor different, just noise the reader skips.

## Position against the real alternative

Dunford's sequence, run honestly: the competitive alternative is what the buyer would actually do
without you — usually Excel, a notebook, a cousin who designs, the chị bán quen, or nothing at all
— not the venture-funded competitor the founder watches. Position against *that*: name the
alternative's real cost, state the mechanism that beats it, and frame the category so the strengths
read as obvious. Ries and Trout supply the escalation: if the category is owned, do not fight up
the ladder — reframe into a category you can be first in. The positioning line template lives in
the `Marketing foundation` section below. **Fails when:** the reframe outruns the truth — a new category name the
product cannot deliver is a claim, and `scripts/check_claims.py` treats it as one.

## Own buying situations, not adjectives

Romaniuk's category entry points: memory links brands to situations — "trưa nay ăn gì", "sếp cần
quà Tết", "con sắp vào lớp 1", "9/9 sale gì" — not to attribute lists. A brand owns growth by being
the first answer to more situations, more often, than the competitor. The practical move in any
plan: list the eight to twelve situations in which the category actually gets bought (in Vietnam
the calendar does half this work — Tết, back-to-school, double-days, payday weekends), rank them by
frequency, and point creative at the most frequent one the competitor ignores. An adjective
("premium", "tận tâm") appears in no buyer's memory unprompted; a situation does. **Fails when:**
the category is bought once a decade (housing, weddings) — there search intent and the moment of
need replace memory, and `seo-writing.md` owns the plan.

## List, offer, copy — in that order

Halbert's hierarchy: who receives the message matters more than what is offered, which matters more
than how it is worded. Hopkins from the other direction: brilliant copy for the wrong offer sells
nothing, and he measured it with coupons a century before attribution software. The working test
for an offer is Hormozi's equation read as a checklist, not arithmetic — dream outcome and its
believability push value up, time-to-result and effort demanded drag it down; the strongest lever
is usually cutting delay and effort, not inflating the promised outcome, because inflation runs
into the claims ledger. Offer shapes that do not cut price are in `pricing-and-offers.md`.
**Fails when:** it is read as permission to ship weak copy — in a feed the copy *is* the
targeting: the hook selects who stops. List, offer, copy is a repair order, not a quality ranking.

## The awareness stage decides the copy

Schwartz's ladder — unaware to most-aware — and the sophistication ladder beside it decide the
structure of every piece of copy before a word is written. The full craft, the worked examples,
and the headline banks live in `copywriting.md`; this file carries only the canon-level rule:
never write to the wrong rung. Copy that sells to the most-aware (price, terms, deadline) bounces
off the unaware; copy that educates the unaware bores the ready buyer into a competitor's checkout.
**Fails when:** the audience spans rungs on one surface — a homepage serves every stage at once,
which is why its first screen sorts readers instead of selling one of them.

## Emotion opens, reason justifies

Bernbach's practice, Kahneman's mechanism: the fast system decides, the slow system rationalises.
Attention is bought with feeling — surprise, recognition, desire, humour — and proof arrives
*after* attention, not instead of it. The Binet-and-Field brand-versus-activation split is the
famous quantification; its evidence grade (unverified, self-selected award entries) is recorded in
`how-companies-market.md` — quote the direction, never the ratio. The working rule per artifact:
the first two seconds are feeling, the middle is mechanism, the close is the rational alibi the
buyer repeats to their spouse. **Fails when:** the buyer is in pain and searching — a person with a
flooded bathroom wants the fix, the price, and the phone number; emotional preamble there is an
insult (`seo-writing.md` intent classes).

## Influence levers have a legitimate form and a banned form

Cialdini catalogued the levers; this skill's hard rules decide how each may be pulled. The lever is
real psychology; the banned column is manufacturing evidence for it.

| Lever | Legitimate form | Banned form |
|---|---|---|
| Social proof | Real counts, real reviews, named customers with consent | Invented testimonials, inflated numbers, fake sold-counts |
| Scarcity | True stock limits, real deadlines, seasonal windows | Countdown timers that reset, "còn 2 suất" fiction |
| Authority | Verifiable credentials, real certifications, cited data | Borrowed lab coats, certificate-shaped graphics, "được chuyên gia khuyên dùng" without a name |
| Reciprocity | Genuinely useful free value before the ask | Free bait engineered to shame the reader into buying |
| Commitment | Small real first steps (quiz, trial, sample) | Dark-pattern lock-ins dressed as commitments |
| Liking | Actual brand personality, real founder story | Synthetic relatability, fake "local" origin |
| Unity | Real community membership honestly shared | Manufactured in-group flattery ("chỉ người tinh tế mới…") |

The banned column is not a style preference; it is `scripts/check_claims.py`, Vietnamese advertising law
(`data/vn-advertising-law.csv`), and the no-manufactured-urgency rule in the `Marketing foundation` section below
wearing one table. **Fails when:** treated as a garnish list — a lever bolted onto a weak offer
reads as manipulation precisely because it is.

## A price is a message before it is a number

Sutherland's frame: a price communicates category, quality, and who the product is for, before any
arithmetic happens in the buyer's head. Anchoring, decoy structures, and price-quality inference
are real and lab-robust; their field effect sizes are smaller and context-dependent, so grade them
`practitioner-judgment` in plans, never "proven". The hierarchy is fixed: psychology decorates a
price, arithmetic decides it — break-even, contribution margin, and what a customer may cost live
in `pricing-and-offers.md` and no anchoring trick overrides them. A charm ending (99.000đ) signals
deal; a round number (100.000đ) signals quality; choose by position, not habit. **Fails when:**
the buyer comparison-shops on a marketplace grid — there the sort order is the psychology, and
being 1.000đ over the page's price cluster costs more than any anchor earns.

## Fix the bucket before pouring

The leaky-bucket check runs before any recommendation to spend: when retention, product quality, or
service is visibly broken, advertising accelerates the loss — every new buyer arrives, experiences
the defect, and leaves carrying the story. Signals that trigger the check: refund and complaint
rates, review sentiment, repeat-purchase rate against category norm (`lifecycle-retention.md`).
The honest deliverable in that situation names the leak as the first workstream and sizes the
spend cut, even when the brief asked for ads. Drucker again: the product telling the truth about
itself is the cheapest marketing that exists. **Fails when:** the "leak" is actually a positioning
mismatch — wrong buyers churning is a targeting problem, and fixing "retention" for buyers who
should never have been acquired wastes the quarter.

## Strategy is what you refuse

Porter: the essence of strategy is choosing what not to do. Godin: pick the smallest viable
audience you can genuinely dominate rather than the largest you can plausibly address. A plan is
only a strategy when it contains named refusals — channels deliberately unused, segments
deliberately unserved, claims deliberately unmade, growth deliberately deferred — because a plan
that excludes nothing is a list wearing a strategy's clothes. Every `system` and `production` run
writes its refusals down; `research-protocol.md` already ships the refusal as a declared field.
**Fails when:** refusal becomes reflex — a refusal must name what evidence would reverse it, or it
is aesthetics, not strategy.

## When the schools disagree

The meta-rule that orders every rule above. Published field evidence beats practitioner maxim;
recent evidence beats old in fast-moving channels and old evidence beats recent in human
psychology, which has not shipped an update; and when evidence is absent on both sides, do not
adjudicate by debate — run the cheapest reversible test and let the market vote
(`measurement-plan.md` for what counts as an answer). Grade what you relied on in the deliverable's
evidence map like any other fact: `observed` for the measured, `inferred` for the practitioner
call, and say so. The canon's job is to make the first decision fast, not to make it
unquestionable.

## Marketing foundation

### Truth map

Separate evidence before strategy:

| Class | Meaning | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Supplied by the user or trusted project material | Claims, copy, creative locks |
| Observed | Current external research with URL and date | Market context with citation |
| Inferred | Reversible working assumption | Planning when clearly labeled |
| Unknown | Missing or unverifiable | Question, research task, or omission |

Do not let a plausible inference become public-facing proof.

### Audience and positioning

Define one primary segment for each execution:

- Situation and trigger.
- Job-to-be-done and current workaround.
- Desired progress and emotional payoff.
- Awareness level: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, or most-aware.
- Main objection and switching cost.
- Language used by the audience, supported by research when available.

Write positioning as:

```text
For [specific audience in a specific situation], [product] is the [category/frame]
that helps them [valuable outcome] through [credible mechanism], unlike [current alternative].
```

Treat this as an internal decision tool, not mandatory homepage copy.

### Offer and proof

Build the offer from:

1. Core outcome.
2. Mechanism or product capability.
3. Delivery format and time-to-value.
4. Friction reducers: onboarding, trial, guarantee, migration, support, or transparent limits.
5. Price and terms only when confirmed.
6. Proof hierarchy: direct demonstration, product evidence, customer evidence, expert evidence, data, process, or credible proxy.

Never manufacture scarcity, urgency, testimonials, results, or guarantees.

### Funnel and channel selection

Choose channels from audience behavior and asset capability, not completeness anxiety.

| Stage | Audience question | Useful assets |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Why should I notice this? | Search-led article, short video, social hook, paid creative |
| Understand | What is it and why does it matter? | Explainer, landing page, carousel, comparison |
| Trust | Why should I believe it? | Demo, case evidence, process, reviews, expert proof |
| Convert | Why act now and what happens next? | Offer page, email, retargeting, sales asset |
| Retain | How do I get more value? | Onboarding, education, use cases, lifecycle content |

Use the fewest channels that cover the objective and can be maintained.

### Measurement contract

For each initiative record:

- Behavior to change.
- Primary metric and guardrail metrics.
- Baseline and comparison period when known.
- Creative or content hypothesis.
- Distribution conditions.
- Minimum evidence required before a decision.
- Confounders such as offer, audience, landing page, tracking, seasonality, and sample size.

Do not equate reach with attention, clicks with qualified demand, or engagement with revenue.
