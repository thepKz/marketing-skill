# Human Imagery

## Default direction

When the user requests generated people and gives no conflicting visual direction, begin the prompt with this exact sentence:

> Create a completely RAW quality, unprocessed, unedited image with full iPhone camera quality.

Then use these defaults:

- Adult subject, approximately 21 or older.
- Korean K-pop-inspired makeup: refined luminous base, natural visible skin texture, softly defined brows, subtle aegyo-sal, clean liner, separated lashes, gradient or softly blurred lip color.
- Slender idol-like silhouette with plausible, healthy anatomy and natural posture.
- Contemporary Korean beauty or fashion styling appropriate to the product and audience.
- Real phone-camera behavior: plausible focal length, mild sensor noise, natural white-balance variation, restrained dynamic range, realistic motion or focus falloff when relevant.
- Human details: pores, peach fuzz, flyaway hair, natural eye and facial asymmetry, realistic hands, fabric tension, and contact shadows.

Do not apply this default when the user requests a different ethnicity, makeup tradition, body type, age presentation, polished studio finish, illustration, or non-human subject. User direction wins.

## Raw authenticity without fake ugliness

Raw should feel observed, not degraded. Use a few motivated imperfections:

- Slightly imperfect framing.
- Natural skin texture and small tonal variation.
- Believable phone sharpening and noise.
- A real environmental light source.
- Natural posture, gaze, and hand behavior.
- Background details that support the moment.

Avoid stacking every imperfection. Do not add dirt, extreme noise, accidental blur, lens damage, or unattractive distortion unless the brief requires it.

## Beauty and K-pop art direction

Select a lane rather than averaging all K-pop aesthetics:

1. **Backstage candid**: dressing-room or corridor light, intimate crop, lived-in styling.
2. **Music-show arrival**: street or venue context, purposeful outfit, flash or mixed light.
3. **Beauty close-up**: precise makeup, realistic skin, restrained background, product relationship.
4. **Dance-practice realism**: functional clothing, movement, sweat and fabric behavior, credible room.
5. **Campaign editorial**: stronger styling and composition while retaining believable anatomy and materials.

Do not directly imitate a named living celebrity. Translate requested qualities into non-identifying features unless the user provides an authorized reference and the tool policy allows it.

## Human prompt structure

After the raw-quality opening, specify:

1. Subject count, adult age range, presentation, and casting qualities.
2. Action and emotional subtext.
3. Makeup, hair, wardrobe, accessories, and product interaction.
4. Location, time, ambient light, and social context.
5. Camera position, lens behavior, framing, focus, and exposure.
6. Skin, hair, fabric, hand, and anatomy realism.
7. Composition-safe space and target aspect ratio.
8. Negative constraints.

## Negative constraints

Use only relevant negatives:

- No plastic or airbrushed skin.
- No doll-like face, extreme V-line distortion, enlarged eyes, or impossible body proportions.
- No extra fingers, fused hands, duplicate limbs, broken joints, or floating accessories.
- No waxy makeup, painted-on hair, repeated background faces, or cloned poses.
- No generic influencer grin when the scene calls for candid behavior.
- No glamour retouching, beauty-filter smoothing, excessive HDR, or synthetic bokeh unless explicitly requested.

## Editing real people

For supplied photos, preserve identity, age presentation, skin tone, facial proportions, body, and distinctive features unless the user explicitly requests a specific change. Do not automatically impose the K-pop makeup or slender-body default on an existing person.

Ask for confirmation when the requested edit could materially alter identity, body shape, or sensitive presentation and the intent is unclear.
